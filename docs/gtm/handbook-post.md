Cahootz works best when people actually use it together. One person posting into an empty room doesn't build much. A few people asking for help, offering what they've got, showing up to events, and voting on what comes next starts to look like a real community.

This handbook walks you through it step by step. Part 1 is for every member. Part 2 is for the people who look after a Commons: stewards, guides, and circle leaders. Along the way you'll see short notes marked **For stewards**. If that's you, read those. If it's not, skip them.

A few words you'll see a lot:

- **Commons:** your group on Cahootz. It could be a neighborhood, a co-op, a congregation, a workplace, or a family.
- **Circle:** a smaller space inside a Commons, like a committee, a project team, or a book club.
- **Sage:** your Commons' AI helper. It's a computer program, not a person. More on Sage in Step 5.
- **Steward:** a member who looks after the Commons: letting people in, managing invitations, and helping it run.

# Part 1: For every member

## Step 1: Get in

Cahootz is an iPhone app. Download it from the App Store and sign in with your email. We'll send you a 6-digit code. There's no password to remember.

Then you join a Commons. There are three ways in:

- **Apply.** Pick a Commons from Browse commons and answer its questions. You'll get a reference number (it looks like APP-XXXXXX). Keep it in case you need to check on your application. A steward reviews it.
- **Get invited.** If someone sent you an invitation, open it and follow the steps. In a family, an invitation lets you in. For other Commons, an invitation is an invitation to apply: you still answer the questions, and a steward still reviews it.
- **Start a family.** Tap **Start a family** to make a private Commons for your family. You'll name it, pick a few shared goals, and invite people.

You can belong to more than one Commons. Switch between them from the top of Circle View, and tap the star on the one you use most so the app opens there.

> **For stewards:** Join requests wait for you on your Commons' **Invite** screen, under "Requests to join."  Try to answer within a day or two. A quick yes is the best welcome there is.

## Step 2: Set up your profile

Before you can start, the app asks for a short intro and at least one interest. If something's missing, the button tells you exactly what.

Take two more minutes on your profile:

- **Add a photo or an emoji.** People are more likely to reply to a face (or a giraffe).
- **Write a real bio.** What do you do? What do you care about? What could you help a neighbor with?
- **Pick a unique @ name.** That's how people mention you in posts.

Your skills and interests matter more than you'd think. When someone in your Commons needs help, Sage looks for members who might be able to help. If it doesn't know you fix bikes, it can't ask you.

## Step 3: Find your way around

When you open the app, you land on **Circle View**. It shows your Commons and the circles you're in. General comes first. That's the main feed everyone in the Commons can see.

Your first stop should be a **welcome lounge**. Tap "Join a welcome lounge," and you'll find Sage's welcome thread asking newcomers to introduce themselves. Say hi: who you are, where you are, and what brought you here. People get an alert when you join, and you'll get one when someone replies to your intro.

From there:

- **Join circles** that fit what you care about. Tap **Manage circles** to see your invitations and the circles you're in.
- **Open the drawer** (top left) for your Commons' Home, Proposals & Votes, Drafts, and About.
- **Check About** to see what your Commons is for: its mission, goals, who's in it, and how decisions get made.

> **For stewards and circle leaders:** If you lead a circle, pin the post you most want people to see, like a meeting time or a how-to. Pinned posts sit at the top with a "Pinned" badge, and members get a highlight alert.

## Step 4: Post, ask, and offer

This is the heart of it. A Commons is only as useful as what people put into it.

- **Ask for what you need.** A ride, a recommendation, a hand moving a couch. The worst that happens is nobody answers, and Sage notices that too (see Step 5).
- **Offer what you've got.** Spare time, a skill, a tool, a room for a meeting.
- **Create events.** Tap **+** in a feed to make one. It shows up in the feed and under "Upcoming." People can RSVP, add it to their calendar, and talk about it.
- **React and reply.** Tap to add any emoji. A ❤️ or 🎉 takes a second and tells someone they were heard.
- **Message people directly.** Open anyone's page and tap **Message** for a private conversation.
- **Mention people** with @ to pull them in. Tap any @name to see that person's page.

You don't need to be clever. "Does anyone know a good tax preparer near downtown?" is a great post.

## Step 5: Meet Sage

Sage is your Commons' AI helper. It reads posts in your Commons to notice what people need, connects people who can help each other, and suggests next steps.

**What Sage does on its own:**

- Replies with helpful information when it's confident it can help
- Sends you reminders and alerts, and checks back on plans
- Asks the whole Commons an open question when the same need keeps coming up
- Posts a regular rhythm in General: a Monday catch-up, midweek open requests, a Friday thank-you based on what actually happened in the app, and a monthly review
- Closes its own suggestions when nobody answers or they no longer apply

**What always waits for a person:** submitting a proposal for you, spending or moving money, changing who's a member, and making decisions for the group.

A few ways to use it:

- **Answer your weekly check-in.** Sage sends one in Messages. Tell it what you need. It saves a short private note about your needs (never your exact words) and uses it to help you later.
- **Message Sage** any time, or type **@sage** in a comment to ask it something in a thread.
- **Ask "What do you remember about me?"** to see what it's saved. Say **"forget everything"** to wipe it.
- **Want to see how it decides?** Go to Profile, then **Sage settings**, and turn on **Show Sage decision trails**. You'll see what Sage read, why it acted, and the checks it ran.

Sage also makes introductions. If you need help and someone else can give it, Sage may offer to introduce you. Nothing happens until **both** of you say yes.

> **For stewards and circle leaders:** When Sage isn't sure enough to reply on its own, it sends the reply to a Commons admin or the circle leader for a yes or no first. Its suggestions in your circle (an event, a post, a proposal idea) come to you too, with "Why Sage suggests this." If an alert isn't really yours to handle, tap **Not for me** and Sage passes it to the next person.

## Step 6: Use the Board

The **Board** is where your Commons keeps track of what people need and offer. It shows:

- **Needs, offers, and events**, with filters so you can see just one kind
- **Marketplace:** shops run by members and shops connected to your Commons
- **Verified resources:** services and places your Commons has checked out

If you're looking for something, check the Board before you post. Someone may already be offering it.

## Step 7: Spend where it counts

Every Commons can have a marketplace of local and member-run shops. Buying there keeps money closer to home.

- **Browse the Marketplace** from the Board, add to your cart, and check out.
- **Pay a shop in person** under **You → Pay a store**. Enter the shop's code (or scan it), type the amount, review it, and pay.
- **Find your receipts** under **You → Your payments**. Tap any payment to see its receipt. Member payments have no fee right now.

**About the coin.** Each Commons has its own coin, with a name the Commons picks. When you buy from a shop marked "Earns rewards," you earn some of that coin. Shops have to apply and be approved before their purchases earn it. The coin is a way to recognize people who support the Commons. It isn't an investment, and you shouldn't buy anything expecting it to make you money.

> **For stewards:** Know a local business that should be in your Marketplace? The owner can start a shop application right from the Marketplace. Once it's approved, they set up payments in the app, add products, and go live. Earning coin rewards is a separate application they can send after that.

## Step 8: Have a say

Your Commons makes decisions through **proposals**, and members vote on them.

**To make a proposal:** open the drawer, tap **Proposals & Votes**, then start a proposal. You answer three plain questions:

1. What do you want to do?
2. Why does it matter, and who does it help?
3. What will it take? (A rough cost, or "Not sure yet," and when.)

That's it. Extra detail, like where, the main steps, and who will help, is folded away if you want it. Sage reviews the proposal against your Commons' goals and charter. It might send it back with notes before it goes to a vote. That's normal. Fix it up and send it again.

**To vote:** open the proposal and pick **Yes, approve**, **No, reject**, or **No opinion**. The app asks you to confirm before it counts. You can change your vote while voting is open. Every proposal shows when voting closes ("Closes in 3 days"), so you won't miss it.

Before a vote closes, Sage may post a short summary of the discussion. It won't tell you how to vote. That's up to each of you.

Sage can also write proposals of its own when one of your Commons' goals is falling behind. They're clearly labeled as coming from Sage, they still go to a member vote, and nothing changes unless members say yes.

## Step 9: Tune your alerts

When you join a circle, its alerts start on **Highlights**: pinned posts, @mentions, and replies to your posts, not every post. Open the circle to switch it to **All activity** or **Only @mentions**. For alert types across the whole app, use **Notification settings**.

Do turn on alerts for:

- Replies to your posts and intros
- New votes in your Commons
- Sage check-ins and introductions

## Step 10: Tell us when something's off

Found a bug? Have an idea? Open the menu and tap **Report a Problem or Idea**. Pick what kind of report it is, tell us what happened, and add a screenshot if it helps. A real person on the team reads every one.

# Part 2: For stewards, guides, and circle leaders

If you look after a Commons or a circle, your job isn't to do everything. It's to make it easy for everyone else to show up. Here's how.

## Know who does what

- **Stewards** look after the Commons. They approve or decline people who ask to join, manage invitations, make others stewards, and remove members. In a family, they also make shareable links and set the goals and agreement.
- **Guides** (in families) can invite people directly, without waiting for a steward. They can't approve requests, change anyone's role, or remove members. It's a good role for your most connected relatives.
- **Circle leaders** are whoever started the circle. They invite people to it, pin posts, and review Sage's suggestions for that circle.

To make someone a steward, open your Commons' **Invite** screen, find them in the member list, and tap **Make steward**. In a family you'll also see **Make guide**. The app spells out what they'll be able to do before you confirm.

## Steward Step 1: Write down what you're for

Sage and your members both work better when the Commons knows what it's trying to do.

- **Set your goals.** For a family, the setup walks you through it: pick goals (or write your own), set an amount and timeframe for each, and decide how much each one matters. The priorities have to add up to 100%.
- **Write your agreement or charter.** Keep it short and plain. What's the Commons for? How do you decide things? What happens when people disagree?

If you skipped this when you started, Steward tools will say "Not set up yet" and offer **Set up goals and agreement**. Do it this week.

Why it matters: Sage measures how your Commons is doing against these goals. When one falls behind, it can propose a fix for members to vote on.

## Steward Step 2: Bring people in

Growth usually comes from people who already trust each other. Make it easy for members to bring friends.

- **Invite people** from the **Invite** screen. Any member can do this. In most Commons it's an invitation to apply, so you'll still review their application.
- **Watch "Requests to join"** and answer quickly.

In a family, you have a few more tools:

- **Make a shareable link** for a group chat or a text. Anyone who opens it can ask to join. They don't get in until you approve.
- **Look at recommendations.** Family members who aren't stewards or guides can **Recommend someone**, and you approve it.
- **Know the phone rule.** An invitation sent to an email address lets the person in once they confirm that email. An invitation sent to a phone number can't be checked the same way, so a steward confirms it's really them.

## Steward Step 3: Make newcomers feel at home

The first week decides whether someone sticks around.

- **Reply to every intro in the welcome lounge.** Even a 🎉 counts. People get an alert when you do.
- **Point newcomers to one circle** that fits what they said they care about.
- **Let Sage and the app help.** Sage posts the welcome thread and gives a shout-out when someone joins, and the app sends quiet newcomers a "Today in…" alert about what's happening. Your job is to be the human who answers.

## Steward Step 4: Build good circles

Circles are where the real work happens. A few habits make them work:

- **Give every circle a clear purpose** when you create it, like "Plan the spring garden workday," not "Garden stuff."
- **Invite people by @ name** from circle settings. Pending invitations stay listed until they accept.
- **Pin what matters:** the meeting time, the sign-up sheet, the current plan.
- **Use the circle gallery** for photos from events. It's the easiest way to show newcomers what the circle actually does.

## Steward Step 5: Work with Sage, not around it

Sage handles a lot of the follow-up that usually burns out organizers. You stay in charge of judgment calls.

- **Review what Sage sends you.** When Sage wants to post something it isn't sure about, or suggests an event or proposal for your circle, you'll get an alert. Approve it, change it, or say no.
- **Pass along what isn't yours.** **Not for me** sends an alert to the next person responsible, and Sage learns from it.
- **Check what Sage is spending.** Open About, then **Overview**, to see Sage's estimated AI spending against your Commons' monthly limit, and whether it's Active or Paused. When it hits the limit, Sage stops acting on its own, but members can keep using the app.
- **Turn on decision trails** in Sage settings if you want to see how Sage reached each decision. It's the best way to build trust in what it's doing, or catch it when it's wrong.

Remember what Sage never does on its own: submit a proposal for a member, spend or move money, change who's a member, or make decisions for the group.

## Steward Step 6: Run decisions people trust

- **Encourage small proposals.** "Buy a folding table for $60" teaches people how voting works better than a big plan does.
- **Talk about proposals in the open.** Comments on a proposal are where people get on the same page. Sage posts a neutral summary before the vote closes.
- **Apply what members pass.** When members vote yes on a charter or goals change, it shows up for stewards under "Changes members passed." Tap **Apply** to make it official. Members can't see that button, so it's on you.
- **Withdraw cleanly.** If a proposal is no longer needed, its author can withdraw it. The app asks first.

## Steward Step 7: A simple weekly routine

About 15 minutes a week keeps a Commons healthy:

- **Monday:** Read Sage's Monday catch-up in General. Answer one open need yourself or tag someone who can.
- **Midweek:** Check "Requests to join" and any alerts from Sage waiting on you.
- **Friday:** Read the Friday thank-you and add your own. Name someone who helped.
- **Once a month:** Look at the monthly review and your goals. Is anything falling behind? Is there a small proposal that would help?

# The short version

- **Members:** fill in your profile, say hi in the welcome lounge, ask for one thing and offer one thing, answer Sage's check-ins, and vote.
- **Stewards:** write down your goals, let people in quickly, answer every intro, keep circles focused, review what Sage sends you, and apply what members pass.

A Commons isn't the app. It's the people who show up in it. The app just makes it easier to keep showing up.
