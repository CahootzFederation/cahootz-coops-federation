# Cahootz Product Thesis

**Status:** Canonical product direction

**Last updated:** November 7, 2026

## Positioning

**Cahootz is the action and economic layer for purpose-driven communities.**

It helps communities turn conversation into shared needs, commitments, decisions,
and completed outcomes. The long-term opportunity is not another social feed. It is
infrastructure that lets a trusted group coordinate its attention, labor, demand,
and money.

## The Problem

Communities already gather in group chats, Facebook Groups, Nextdoor, email lists,
and meetings. Those tools help people talk, but the work usually breaks down after
the conversation:

- needs and offers disappear in a feed;
- nobody owns the next step;
- commitments, deadlines, and decisions are unclear;
- members cannot easily see what was completed;
- shared demand and spending power remain fragmented.

Cahootz exists to close that gap between **conversation and coordinated action**.

## Who It Is For

The initial customer is an existing purpose-driven community with:

- a trusted core of members;
- a reason to act together repeatedly;
- needs, offers, projects, or purchases that are currently coordinated manually;
- at least one organizer responsible for keeping activity moving.

Examples include mutual-aid networks, churches, cultural organizations,
neighborhood initiatives, professional communities, cooperatives, and member-led
economic groups.

The initial goal is not to attract an undifferentiated public audience. Cahootz
should help a specific community become measurably more effective.

## Core Member Job

> Help my community turn something we care about into a clear commitment and a
> completed result, without losing the people, context, or accountability along
> the way.

## Core Product Loop

1. **Gather:** A person joins a Commons and finds the people relevant to them.
2. **Express:** Members share a need, an offer, an opportunity, or an idea.
3. **Commit:** The group turns interest into explicit commitments, ownership, and
   a next step.
4. **Coordinate:** Members decide, assign work, pool resources, and follow progress.
5. **Complete:** The group verifies what happened and records the outcome.
6. **Compound:** Visible outcomes strengthen trust, participation, and the group's
   capacity to act again.

Every major feature should strengthen at least one step in this loop without making
the full loop harder to complete.

## Product Wedge

The entry product should feel familiar enough to replace a Facebook Group,
Nextdoor group, or fragmented group chat for an existing community:

- Commons and smaller circles;
- member profiles and invitations;
- focused community feeds and discussion;
- structured asks, offers, and opportunities;
- lightweight commitments, ownership, follow-up, and visible outcomes.

The differentiator is not a better feed. It is that useful conversations can become
trackable action without moving to spreadsheets, separate payment links, and more
group chats.

## Economic Expansion

Once communities repeatedly complete non-financial actions, Cahootz can help them
coordinate economic power through:

- aggregated demand and group purchasing;
- member and local-business marketplaces;
- shared funds, dues, and project financing;
- cooperative storefronts and payments;
- transparent governance and treasury workflows.

Economic features should attach to demonstrated member demand. They should not be
used to manufacture engagement before a community has a reason to act together.

## Product Principles

1. **Outcomes over activity.** Posts, comments, and votes are inputs; completed
   community outcomes are the goal.
2. **Start with existing trust.** Activate real communities before pursuing a broad
   consumer network.
3. **Structure only when useful.** Make conversation easy, then add the minimum
   structure needed to create ownership and follow-through.
4. **Human authority is explicit.** AI may notice, recommend, draft, route, and
   follow up. Authorized people make governance, financial, disciplinary, and other
   high-impact decisions.
5. **Verify in proportion to the stakes.** Use simple, low-friction systems for
   ordinary social coordination and stronger audit, authorization, or cryptographic
   guarantees for money, governance, identity, and irreversible actions.
6. **Serve varied communities.** Governance models, roles, thresholds, and economic
   rules must be configurable rather than imposed as universal Cahootz ideology.
7. **Earn expansion.** Add economic complexity after communities demonstrate
   repeat participation and completed action.

## Non-Goals

Cahootz is not trying to be:

- a generic social network optimized for attention or time spent;
- a crypto product that requires every community action to be on-chain;
- an autonomous AI governor or substitute for member authority;
- a universal governance constitution imposed on every Commons;
- a marketplace with no underlying community relationship;
- a collection of disconnected co-op tools without a coherent member loop.

## Product Success

The primary product measure is **verified completed actions per active Commons**.
An action counts only when it has an owner or participating members, a defined
intended outcome, and a recorded completion state that authorized participants can
confirm.

Supporting measures include:

- invite-to-activated-member conversion;
- percentage of active Commons with at least one ask or offer;
- ask/offer-to-commitment conversion;
- commitment-to-verified-completion rate;
- median time from expressed need to first commitment;
- repeat participation after a completed outcome;
- cost per completed outcome for Sage-assisted workflows;
- later, aggregated demand fulfilled and member dollars coordinated.

Raw registrations, posts, comments, votes, AI outputs, and transaction volume are
diagnostics, not proof that Cahootz is working.

## Decision Test

Before starting a major feature, answer:

1. Which part of the core loop does it strengthen?
2. What real community behavior or evidence justifies it?
3. What completed outcome should improve if it works?
4. Is this the smallest version that can test that claim?
5. Does it preserve human authority and security in proportion to the stakes?

If those answers are unclear, the feature is not ready to build.
